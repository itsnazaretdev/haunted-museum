## Life

IBM started working on the floppy disk in 1967 and introduced it commercially in 1971, as a big
8-inch flexible disk. Then it shrank: in 1976 Shugart Associates launched the 5¼-inch version,
which became almost universal on word processors and personal computers.

In 1982 Sony introduced the 3½-inch design, the hard plastic square with a metal shutter that
most people remember. In 1987 the high-density version arrived with its famous "1.44 MB"
label, even though the real formatted capacity was 1,440 KiB.

For years it was how people carried homework, installed programs, shared games and saved
everything.

## Death

By the mid-to-late 1990s its use started to decline, and after 2000 floppy disks were rare,
used mostly with older hardware. CDs, USB drives and later the internet took its place.
Sony manufactured its last new floppy disks in 2011.

## Defense

It made personal computing portable. Before the cloud, before USB, the floppy disk was the way
to move your work from one computer to another. And its shape lives on as the Save icon.

## Why it should come back

It argues that a file you can hold in your hand is the most private cloud there is: no account,
no server, no subscription.
