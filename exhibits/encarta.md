## Life

Microsoft launched Encarta in 1993, a multimedia encyclopedia on CD-ROM. It had articles,
pictures, sounds, videos and interactive maps, something that felt magical on a home computer.

For many students it was the first place to look for information, and lots of school projects
were made with text and pictures from Encarta.

## Death

The web changed how people looked for information. With Wikipedia and search engines, a paid
encyclopedia on a disc felt old. On March 30, 2009, Microsoft announced it would stop Encarta.
It stopped selling the software in June 2009 and the Encarta websites closed on October 31, 2009.

## Defense

It made knowledge fun and multimedia when the internet was still slow and rare in most homes.
It worked perfectly offline.

## Why it should come back

It argues that an encyclopedia that works with no internet at all is still a good idea, and
that its spirit lives in every offline, local tool that keeps knowledge on your own machine.
