## Life

VHS was developed by JVC in the 1970s. It fought a famous format war against Sony's Betamax and
won, becoming the standard way to watch and record video at home.

For years, families recorded TV shows, birthdays and holidays on VHS tapes, and Friday nights
meant going to the video store to rent a movie. Rental tapes often had a sticker reminding
people: "Be kind, rewind."

## Death

DVDs arrived with better quality and no rewinding, and later streaming changed everything.
In July 2016, Funai Electric, the last company still making VCRs, stopped production because of
falling demand and trouble finding parts.

## Defense

VHS brought movies into people's homes and let normal families record their own memories. Many
of those home videos are the only recordings of people who are no longer here.

## Why it should come back

It argues that some memories deserve to be held, labeled by hand and kept on a shelf, not
hidden in a streaming catalog that can change any day.
